% Abstract (Do not insert blank lines, i.e. \\) 
\abstract{This paper addresses the problem of mapping land cover types in Senegal and recognition of vegetation systems in the Saloum Delta River on the satellite images. In monitoring of landscape dynamics, the goal of the remote sensing approach is to find all the identical landscape patterns on a time series of images and to analyse changes using recognition of landscape patches. Multi-seasonal landscape dynamics in the region of Saloum River was analysed on the basis of Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS datasets on 2014 to 2023 and GRASS GIS scripts using image analysis techniques. Contrary to the existing GIS, programming approach to satellite image processing has a high automation in data handling and is very fast and robust to classify and to compare the satellite images. This is achieved by a combination of the embedded GRASS GIS modules used separately for computing the vegetation indices and plotting the classification maps. The land cover types were identified based on the analysis of attributed of the spectral reflectance of the multispectral images. The detected land cover changes indicate climate change factors and the effects of environmental disasters in semi-arid region of West Africa: variability of precipitation, increase in temperature, land erosions and wildfire. These factors control the distribution of croplands, natural vegetation systems and coastal mangrove colonies in western Senegal. The results based on the observed satellite images indicated the decrease in savannahs, increase in croplands and agricultural lands, decline in forests and changes in coastal wetlands including mangroves with high biodiversity. Mapping land cover types, discriminating mangroves and monitoring savannah ecosystems is essential for proper land management of Senegal. This paper contributed to the natural resource management and environmental monitoring of Senegal through applied advanced cartographic methods to remote sensing Earth observation data on Senegal, West Africa.
}

\subsection{Background}
Land cover types (or land use types) refer to physical surface components of Earth cover that physically present and visible on the landscapes. Compared to land use types, which are defined as landscape patches that are used by the people, land cover types are typically representing physical characteristics of the Earth's surface in terms of structure, patterns and components. Due to these factors, the identification of land cover types is possible using analysis and synthesis of remote sensing data such as spaceborne images. Hence, the environmental studies often use the products of Landsat mission for landscape analysis. Major types of satellite images for effective interpretation of Earth's landscapes include Sentinel 2 Data \cite{Ngom,TONG2020111598}, NOAA-AVHRR \cite{KONARSKA2002491,ANYAMBA2005596}, SPOT \cite{SILVA2005188,MARTINEZ2011152}, or Landsat \cite{PIMPLE2020118007,Lemenkova202315},  and the combinations thereof \cite{rs12193157}. Landsat scenes used for landscape mapping include the data acquired from various Landsat sensors such as Multispectral Scanner (MSS), Thematic Mapper (TM), Enhanced Thematic Mapper Plus (ETM+) and recent product of Operational Land Imager and Thermal Infrared Sensor (OLI/TIRS).

Certainly, although diverse images can be used in various combination as data sources for landscape interpretation and environmental mapping, the general approach of remote sensing data processing relies on using the data on spectral reflectance obtained as values of pixels on the image scene \cite{ACKER2014}. The effects of reflectance and absorption of various land cover types in different wavelengths, i.e., the variations in brightness, colour primaries (red, green, blue), hue, saturation and intensity of light enables to discriminate major land cover types on the multispectral images: water, land, forests (with various types of vegetation), urban zones, desert sands, etc \cite{rs12101634}. Such phenomena was used in experimental studies where spectral reflectance were used in order to evaluate the land/water borders and discriminate them from other land cover types, and thus to enhance the recognition of Earth's objects \cite{rs13132454}. Such information is provided by the
environmental descriptors and can be obtained from the analysis of values of pixels recording spectral reflectance of diverse land cover types which is derived from the combination of different bands in multispectral imagery, e.g., NIR/Red bands for computing the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) and other vegetation indices \cite{CRIPPEN199071,LI2004463,jmse11040871,RUAN2022157075}.

\subsection{Problem formulation}

One of the challenging problem in identification of land cover types on the satellite images is how to automatically extract reliable features in image scene, especially highly heterogeneous landscape patches in complex mosaic of intermittent land cover types \cite{CAMPOS2018211,SYIFA2020919}. With this regard, image processing and analysis for landscape interpretation is a fundamental issue in environmental mapping and thematic cartography. Identification of land cover types on the satellite images implies diverse techniques of image processing such as classification, clustering and segmentation. Image analysis is especially essential for classification of mixed terrestrial ecoregions such as those in Senegal \cite{BUDDE2004481}. The landscape of Senegal are notable for a complex mix of diverse semi-arid landscapes such as forest-savanna mosaics, intermittent with Sahelian Acacia and West Sudanian savanna with diverse tree species, as well as coastal regions with dominating Guinean mangroves \cite{TAPPAN2004427,DEANS2003253}.

Given the difficulty of automatic feature extraction on the processed images, a manual markup of various features in analysed landscapes, such as regions of interest (ROI), singular patches and landscape corridors, is the current practice \cite{CABRAL2017115}. However, this human factor issue in landscape examination has raised some concerns related to repeatability and reliability which suggested the development of automatic methods of image interpretation and objects recognition \cite{CONCHEDDA2008578,rs9020181,rs12091436}. Specifically, manual interpretation of land cover types can cause misclassification of pixels on the images which leads to misinterpretation of selected landscape patches. Quantitative data interpretation for prediction and analysis are difficult to implement in African countries with limited data, as noted earlier \cite{STOORVOGEL2009161}. Therefore, the use of satellite data processed with high level of automation for mapping landscape properties plays an essential role in remote sensing based vegetation mapping and agricultural monitoring \cite{PEREZHOYOS2020102064,BRANDT2016215}.  

The key factors in effective image analysis include the type of classifiers and the algorithms used for data handling \cite{Lemenkova202212}. Another important factor consists in the quality of imagery for the evaluated ROI in study area (e.g., minimal cloudiness and haze, correct time of image capture, image coverage, etc). Finally, the essential factor in image analysis includes the methodology and approaches to image processing including the major steps of data acquisition, preprocessing, description, recognition, classification, segmentation, interpretation and accuracy analysis. The growth of methods of image processing during the development of computer-based methods and programming approaches are the primary factors of gain in volume and success of multiple examples of image processing for landscape studies \cite{Duncan,jimaging9050098,rs13101961,info14040249,DIOUF2001129}. Developing the advanced tools of image processing and related cartographic tasks results in many approaches to utilizing effective methods of automated image analysis and synthesis for interpretation of vegetation patterns \cite{SUOMALAINEN2021112691,OlsonAnderson,Lemenkova202227,Ali,Olsonetal2019,Lemenkova202229}.

\subsection{Objective and Motivation}

In this study, we explore the advanced approaches of remote sensing data processing by the GRASS GIS scripting techniques to classify land cover types in the coastal region of west Senegal using a series of Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS satellite images. The images cover recent period of 2015 to 2023. The aim of this study is to map and evaluate changes in various land cover types of west Senegal, which include both inner regions and coastal mangrove forests in the delta of Saloum River, Figure \ref{fig01}. Recent studies reported a drastic loss in mangrove ecosystems which are currently experiencing effects from climate changes and anthropogenic activities \cite{ANDRIEU2020117963} as well as local effects of soil salinity \cite{Devaney}. Understanding the dynamics of these unique wetland ecosystems is possible using spatial analysis of the remote sensing data which enables to evaluate  gains and losses in landscape patches.

The drought period in Africa in the 1970s and 1980s resulted in climate changes in Africa with such consequences as decreased rainfall and precipitation, raised temperatures and increased evaporation \cite{Paturel,Lemenkova202217}. Such as other processes triggered land cover changes in natural vegetation patterns of Sahel ecoregions which affected cultivated areas and croplands, increased spots of bare soil, and changes in surface water \cite{Fritz,Lemenkova202313}. With this regard, one of the priorities of image processing methods is to support environmental landscape monitoring through interpretation of land cover types using time series analysis which enables to detect environmental changes, land and soil degradation and  variations in vegetation cover \cite{MBOW2008210}. Another essential application of image analysis is monitoring coastal zones for evaluating the hydrological changes, extent of river flooding in delta and detection erosion \cite{NDOUR201892,NGOM201634}, or monitoring degradation of mangroves and coastal ecosystems \cite{LOMBARD2023102027,CARNEY2014126}

The is a rich body of literature on exemplar land cover changes in Senegal \cite{WOOD2004565,ELBERLING200337,Hiraldo}, effects of climate change on landscape variability and vegetation communities \cite{LIU2004583,TSCHAKERT2004535,LUFAFA20081}, environmental dynamics and sustainability \cite{CAMARA2017204}. But these approaches do not consider neither apply the advantages of satellite image processing using scripts which significantly increase the speed and automation of mapping ensuring the accuracy and objectivity of land cover classification. Indeed, image processing using GRASS GIS partitions the image into structured land patches using automatic analysis of spectral reflectance of the pixels and assigns them into various classes using principles of machine based vision and pattern recognition. Such approach makes it advantageous to visualise and estimate changes in land cover types using images on different years as time series analysis. Besides, the traditional software used for image processing entails drawbacks while data processing. 

To address the main difficulties in traditional image processing, namely presence of structured noise on classified scenes, poor quality or images related to misinterpretation of pixels and low speed of data handling, we propose a script-based classification and image enhancement technique to analyse land cover changes in the coastal region of West Senegal. The main contribution of this paper is as follows: 

\begin{itemize}
  \item A combination of the GRASS GIS modules 'i.group', 'i.maxlik' and 'i.cluster' are proposed for image classification. The land cover types are classified based on the difference in spectral reflectance of the pixels on the images;
  \item The land cover types in the coastal Senegal are defined as the groups of pixels according to the structural similarity between the landscape patches. Orientation, location and frequency of mangrove fields in the Saloum River Delta are estimated from the analysis of landscape patches in the coastal area;
  \item To balance robustness and accuracy, a coarse to fine strategy is proposed which uses the interpretation of landscape patches using FAO scheme of land cover types for the whole country, downscaled to the ROI of the selected study area with images covering the region of Cape Verde Peninsula and the surrounding territories;
  \item The proposed image processing and classification algorithms utilising the GRASS GIS techniques outperforms existing traditional software with GUI approaches in terms of repeatability of scripts and automation which is useful for future similar studies that continue the land cover type analysis in Senegal.
\end{itemize}

\section{Materials and Methods}

To increase the speed and accuracy of image processing, the Geographic Resources Analysis Support System Geographic Information System (GRASS GIS) software was applied as an advanced image processing software with extended functionalities \cite{Neteleretal2008}. The Generic Mapping Tools (GMT) Version 6 \cite{Wessel} is used as a cartographic tool for topographic mapping of the study area and an R sofwtare was applied for plotting the flowchart diagram showing the major research steps.  Both the cartographic and remote sensing parts of the work are based on utilising scripting techniques which main advantages include the high speed of daha processing and the repeatability of scripts, as explained in earlier works \cite{min13050604,Lemenkova202213,Lemenkova202206}.

\section{Results and Discussion}

The analysis of the classified images shows a trend in land cover dynamics of the coastal region of Senegal over Dakar and surrounding regions. The results demonstrate that the dominating land cover type of Senegal remain savannah (73\%) although the areas covered by savannah showed a slight decrease since 2015. The landscapes covered by the croplands and agricultural areas, in contrast, expanded to 22\%. Furthermore, the decline in the areas covered by forests and mangroves are notable. Nevertheless, in general, the savannahs, woodlands, and forests remain the dominating land cover types in Senegal covering over 75\% of the country, which correspond to the similar studies \cite{TAPPAN2004427}.
