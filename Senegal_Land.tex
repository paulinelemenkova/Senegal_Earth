% Abstract (Do not insert blank lines, i.e. \\) 
\abstract{This paper addresses the problem of mapping land cover types in Senegal and recognition of vegetation systems in the Saloum Delta River on the satellite images. In monitoring of landscape dynamics, the goal of the remote sensing approach is to find all the identical landscape patterns on a time series of images and to analyse changes using recognition of landscape patches. Multi-seasonal landscape dynamics in the region of Saloum River was analysed on the basis of Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS datasets on 2014 to 2023 and GRASS GIS scripts using image analysis techniques. Contrary to the existing GIS, programming approach to satellite image processing has a high automation in data handling and is very fast and robust to classify and to compare the satellite images. This is achieved by a combination of the embedded GRASS GIS modules used separately for computing the vegetation indices and plotting the classification maps. The land cover types were identified based on the analysis of attributed of the spectral reflectance of the multispectral images. The detected land cover changes indicate climate change factors and the effects of environmental disasters in semi-arid region of West Africa: variability of precipitation, increase in temperature, land erosions and wildfire. These factors control the distribution of croplands, natural vegetation systems and coastal mangrove colonies in western Senegal. The results based on the observed satellite images indicated the decrease in savannahs, increase in croplands and agricultural lands, decline in forests and changes in coastal wetlands including mangroves with high biodiversity. Mapping land cover types, discriminating mangroves and monitoring savannah ecosystems is essential for proper land management of Senegal. This paper contributed to the natural resource management and environmental monitoring of Senegal through applied advanced cartographic methods to remote sensing Earth observation data on Senegal, West Africa.
}

\section{Introduction}

%----------- subsection ----------->
\subsection{Background}
Land cover types (or land use types) refer to physical surface components of Earth cover that physically present and visible on the landscapes. Compared to land use types, which are defined as landscape patches that are used by the people, land cover types are typically representing physical characteristics of the Earth's surface in terms of structure, patterns and components. Due to these factors, the identification of land cover types is possible using analysis and synthesis of remote sensing data such as spaceborne images. Hence, the environmental studies often use the products of Landsat mission for landscape analysis. Major types of satellite images for effective interpretation of Earth's landscapes include Sentinel 2 Data \cite{Ngom,TONG2020111598}, NOAA-AVHRR \cite{KONARSKA2002491,ANYAMBA2005596,Frederiksen}, SPOT \cite{SILVA2005188,MARTINEZ2011152}, or Landsat \cite{PIMPLE2020118007,Lemenkova202315}, and the combinations thereof \cite{rs12193157}. For instance, recent Global Land Cover 2000 map was based on the analysis of the Vegetation sensor on-board the SPOT-4 satellite using methods of digital image processing and GIS \cite{Mayaux}. Landsat scenes used for landscape mapping include the data acquired from various Landsat sensors such as Multispectral Scanner (MSS), Thematic Mapper (TM), Enhanced Thematic Mapper Plus (ETM+) and recent product of Operational Land Imager and Thermal Infrared Sensor (OLI/TIRS).

Certainly, although diverse images can be used in various combination as data sources for landscape interpretation and environmental mapping, the general approach of remote sensing data processing relies on using the data on spectral reflectance obtained as values of pixels on the image scene \cite{ACKER2014}. The effects of reflectance and absorption of various land cover types in different wavelengths, i.e., the variations in brightness, colour primaries (red, green, blue), hue, saturation and intensity of light enables to discriminate major land cover types on the multispectral images: water, land, forests (with various types of vegetation), urban zones, desert sands, etc \cite{rs12101634}. Such phenomena was used in experimental studies where spectral reflectance were used in order to evaluate the land/water borders and discriminate them from other land cover types, and thus to enhance the recognition of Earth's objects \cite{rs13132454}. Such information is provided by the
environmental descriptors and can be obtained from the analysis of values of pixels recording spectral reflectance of diverse land cover types which is derived from the combination of different bands in multispectral imagery, e.g., NIR/Red bands for computing the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) and other vegetation indices \cite{CRIPPEN199071,LI2004463,jmse11040871,RUAN2022157075}.

\subsection{Problem formulation}

One of the challenging problem in identification of land cover types on the satellite images is how to automatically extract reliable features in image scene, especially highly heterogeneous landscape patches in complex mosaic of intermittent land cover types \cite{CAMPOS2018211,SYIFA2020919}. With this regard, image processing and analysis for landscape interpretation is a fundamental issue in environmental mapping and thematic cartography. Identification of land cover types on the satellite images implies diverse techniques of image processing such as classification, clustering and segmentation. Image analysis is especially essential for classification of mixed terrestrial ecoregions such as those in Senegal \cite{BUDDE2004481}. The landscape of Senegal are notable for a complex mix of diverse semi-arid landscapes such as forest-savanna mosaics, intermittent with Sahelian Acacia and West Sudanian savanna with diverse tree species, as well as coastal regions with dominating Guinean mangroves \cite{TAPPAN2004427,DEANS2003253}.

Given the difficulty of automatic feature extraction on the processed images, a manual markup of various features in analysed landscapes, such as regions of interest (ROI), singular patches and landscape corridors, is the current practice \cite{CABRAL2017115}. However, this human factor issue in landscape examination has raised some concerns related to repeatability and reliability which suggested the development of automatic methods of image interpretation and objects recognition \cite{CONCHEDDA2008578,rs9020181,rs12091436}. Specifically, manual interpretation of land cover types can cause misclassification of pixels on the images which leads to misinterpretation of selected landscape patches. Quantitative data interpretation for prediction and analysis are difficult to implement in African countries with limited data, as noted earlier \cite{STOORVOGEL2009161}. Therefore, the use of satellite data processed with high level of automation for mapping landscape properties plays an essential role in remote sensing based vegetation mapping and agricultural monitoring \cite{PEREZHOYOS2020102064,BRANDT2016215}.  

The key factors in effective image analysis include the type of classifiers and the algorithms used for data handling \cite{Lemenkova202212}. Another important factor consists in the quality of imagery for the evaluated ROI in study area (e.g., minimal cloudiness and haze, correct time of image capture, image coverage, etc). Finally, the essential factor in image analysis includes the methodology and approaches to image processing including the major steps of data acquisition, preprocessing, description, recognition, classification, segmentation, interpretation and accuracy analysis. The growth of methods of image processing during the development of computer-based methods and programming approaches are the primary factors of gain in volume and success of multiple examples of image processing for landscape studies \cite{Duncan,jimaging9050098,Purkis,info14040249,DIOUF2001129}. Developing the advanced tools of image processing and related cartographic tasks results in many approaches to utilizing effective methods of automated image analysis and synthesis for interpretation of vegetation patterns \cite{SUOMALAINEN2021112691,OlsonAnderson,Lemenkova202227,Ali,Olsonetal2019,Lemenkova202229}.

\subsection{Objective and Motivation}

In this study, we explore the advanced approaches of remote sensing data processing by the GRASS GIS scripting techniques to classify land cover types in the coastal region of west Senegal using a series of Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS satellite images. The images cover recent period of 2015 to 2023. The aim of this study is to map and evaluate changes in various land cover types of west Senegal, which include both inner regions and coastal mangrove forests in the delta of Saloum River, Figure \ref{fig01}. Recent studies reported a drastic loss in mangrove ecosystems which are currently experiencing effects from climate changes and anthropogenic activities \cite{ANDRIEU2020117963} as well as local effects of soil salinity \cite{Devaney}. Understanding the dynamics of these unique wetland ecosystems is possible using spatial analysis of the remote sensing data which enables to evaluate  gains and losses in landscape patches.

The drought period in Africa in the 1970s and 1980s resulted in climate changes in Africa with such consequences as decreased rainfall and precipitation, raised temperatures and increased evaporation \cite{Paturel,Lemenkova202217}. Such as other processes triggered land cover changes in natural vegetation patterns of Sahel ecoregions which affected cultivated areas and croplands, increased spots of bare soil, and changes in surface water \cite{Fritz,Lemenkova202313}. With this regard, one of the priorities of image processing methods is to support environmental landscape monitoring through interpretation of land cover types using time series analysis which enables to detect environmental changes, land and soil degradation and  variations in vegetation cover \cite{MBOW2008210}. Another essential application of image analysis is monitoring coastal zones for evaluating the hydrological changes, extent of river flooding in delta and detection erosion \cite{NDOUR201892,NGOM201634}, or monitoring degradation of mangroves and coastal ecosystems \cite{LOMBARD2023102027,CARNEY2014126}

The is a rich body of literature on exemplar land cover changes in Senegal \cite{WOOD2004565,ELBERLING200337,Hiraldo}, effects of climate change on landscape variability and vegetation communities \cite{LIU2004583,TSCHAKERT2004535,LUFAFA20081}, environmental dynamics and sustainability \cite{CAMARA2017204}. But these approaches do not consider neither apply the advantages of satellite image processing using scripts which significantly increase the speed and automation of mapping ensuring the accuracy and objectivity of land cover classification. Indeed, image processing using GRASS GIS partitions the image into structured land patches using automatic analysis of spectral reflectance of the pixels and assigns them into various classes using principles of machine based vision and pattern recognition. Such approach makes it advantageous to visualise and estimate changes in land cover types using images on different years as time series analysis. Besides, the traditional software used for image processing entails drawbacks while data processing. 

To address the main difficulties in traditional image processing, namely presence of structured noise on classified scenes, poor quality or images related to misinterpretation of pixels and low speed of data handling, we propose a script-based classification and image enhancement technique to analyse land cover changes in the coastal region of West Senegal. The main contribution of this paper is as follows: 

\begin{itemize}
  \item A combination of the GRASS GIS modules 'i.group', 'i.maxlik' and 'i.cluster' are proposed for image classification. The land cover types are classified based on the difference in spectral reflectance of the pixels on the images;
  \item The land cover types in the coastal Senegal are defined as the groups of pixels according to the structural similarity between the landscape patches. Orientation, location and frequency of mangrove fields in the Saloum River Delta are estimated from the analysis of landscape patches in the coastal area;
  \item To balance robustness and accuracy, a coarse to fine strategy is proposed which uses the interpretation of landscape patches using FAO scheme of land cover types for the whole country, downscaled to the ROI of the selected study area with images covering the region of Cape Verde Peninsula and the surrounding territories;
  \item The proposed image processing and classification algorithms utilising the GRASS GIS techniques outperforms existing traditional software with GUI approaches in terms of repeatability of scripts and automation which is useful for future similar studies that continue the land cover type analysis in Senegal.
\end{itemize}

\section{Materials and Methods}

To increase the speed and accuracy of image processing, the Geographic Resources Analysis Support System Geographic Information System (GRASS GIS) software was applied as an advanced image processing software with extended functionalities \cite{Neteleretal2008}. The Generic Mapping Tools (GMT) Version 6 \cite{Wessel} is used as a cartographic tool for topographic mapping of the study area and an R software was applied for plotting the flowchart diagram showing the major research steps.  Both the cartographic and remote sensing parts of the work are based on utilising scripting techniques which main advantages include the high speed of data processing and the repeatability of scripts, as explained in earlier works \cite{min13050604,Lemenkova202213,Lemenkova202206}.

We use six Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS images for evaluation of land cover changes in western Senegal during 2015 to 2023, with major details summarised in Table \ref{tab01}, and technical characteristics of the images provided in the tables of the Appendix \ref{App01}. The images were downloaded from the United States Geological Survey (USGS) EarthExplorer repository, Figure \ref{fig02}. The data contain scenes taken during the winter period (January/February) which enables to better detect plant phenology in semi-arid climate, since summer months are not suitable for plant detection due to high level of droughts in desert areas in the inner regions of the country. Besides, the images were selected using cloudiness and haze below 10\% which restricted the use of images on spring/autumn time. Hence, the best quality and suitability of images was on winter period (January/February).

\subsection{Methodology}

The methodology of this study uses three scripting software for data processing, with all the programming scripts shown in the Appendix \ref{App2} section of this study, for technical reference.

 \begin{enumerate}
        \item GRASS GIS script was used for image processing, Listing \ref{lst01}; 
        \item GMT script was used for plotting the topography of the country, Listing \ref{lst02};  
        \item R script was used for plotting the methodological flowchart, Listing \ref{lst03}. 
  \end{enumerate}       

 The main idea was to apply in a single workflow framework both for the image processing and the cartographic mapping using scripts run from the console. This enabled to reveal the technical performance of automated geospatial data processing using programming approach of three various programming suits from the script-based software. Such approach demonstrated in this paper show that the inclusion of scripts speeds up cartographic plotting and image processing and summarises the research methodology. As mentioned earlier \cite{Lemenkova202215}, the main advantage of scripts consists in automation of the process and repeatability of the workflow for future studies. In this way, it enables to significantly save the time due to automation which is achieved by scripts. Then, using the information received from the automated image analysis and interpretation we were able to detect the changes in the analysis landscapes of western Senegal for evaluation of environmental effects from climate change through the series of the images and analysed remote sensing data taken on different years (2015-2023). 

The workflow of the data processing and major methodological steps are shown in Figure \ref{fig04}. After preprocessing and data import, the images were grouped to select only the necessary raster bands using the 'i.group' module using GRASS GIS functionality \cite{NetelerMitasova2008}. Afterwards, the images were clustered using the clustering procedure by module 'i.cluster' which assigned pixels to the groups for which their spectral reflectance was measured automatically. Such image processing provided enough data to group the pixels into land cover types and to evaluate the values of pixels at the actual scenes for comparison.

The algorithm of data processing was implemented in the GRASS GIS software \cite{NETELER2012124}, and run on the Mac machine with Apple M1 chip and macOS operating system. The average computation time for image classification and enhancement per Landsat scene is about 2.5 seconds. The ultimate goal of classification and image interpretation is to detect changes in land cover types over the period of 2015-2023. The matching performance of landscape patches over this period is evaluated using GRASS GIS functionality as a sequence of modules utilised for processing each scene separately using script. The images were read into the GRASS GIS environment using the 'r.import' module; the list of the available bands was checked using the 'g.list rast' command. The extent (UTM Zone 28) and resolution were set to the region of current image which was detected automatically along with geospatial data (coordinates, location, projection, etc). The False colour composites were plotted to clearly distinguish the area of vegetation of mangroves in the delta of the River Saloum (coloured in red shadows), land (coloured by beige and light brownish shadows) and water area (coloured black for the seawater and dark navy for the River Saloum), Figure \ref{fig05}.

\section{Results and Discussion}

The analysis of the classified images shows a trend in land cover dynamics of the coastal region of Senegal over Dakar and surrounding regions, Figure \ref{fig06}. The results demonstrate that the dominating land cover type of Senegal remain savannah (73\%) although the areas covered by savannah showed a slight decrease since 2015. The landscapes covered by the croplands and agricultural areas, in contrast, expanded to 22\%. Furthermore, the decline in the areas covered by forests and mangroves are notable. Nevertheless, in general, the savannahs, woodlands, and forests remain the dominating land cover types in Senegal covering over 75\% of the country, which correspond to the similar studies \cite{TAPPAN2004427}. 

A special focus of this region is laid on mangrove swamplands which are a crucial ecosystems for Senegal, a source of natural resources \cite{Carney} and a habitat for species \cite{Galat}. At the same time, diverse factors have strongly correlated with mangrove losses. These include climate-related processes such as changes in temperatures and precipitation, flooding, and increased salinisation of waters, and human-related factors such as increased processes of intensive urbanisation \cite{land10100996} and active agriculture practices \cite{Branoff}. Overall, the analysis of land cover types over the coastal areas shows the variability in mangroves (coloured bright cyan in Figure \ref{fig06}) which is explained by the high interannual fluctuations in the delta of Saloum River. Thus, the upper tidal zone is flooded during the high tides. In contrast, during the drought period, the coastal area remains drained and subject to evaporation which leads to the salinisation of soil.

The data on the land cover types in Senegal at the country level were plotted and visualised in the QGIS software using the Environmental Systems Research Institute (ESRI) format vector layers, Figure \ref{fig07}. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO)/United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) classification scheme, the land cover types were defined on the images as 10 major classes, adopted for the study area and generalised from the whole country level using the FAO/UNEP Land Cover Classification System (LCCS): 

 \begin{enumerate}
        \item Mosaic grassland or shrubland / cropland; 
        \item Mosaic vegetation / cropland; 
        \item Mangrove wetlands; 
        \item Rainfed cropland; 
        \item Consolidated bare areas;
        \item Mosaic grassland/forest or shrubland; 
        \item Closed broadleaved forest or shrubland permanently flooded - brackish water; 
        \item Salt hardpans; 
        \item Post flooding or irrigated or aquatic cropland; 
        \item Water bodies. 
\end{enumerate}

The quantitative estimations of changes in land cover types over the evaluated period (2015 to 2023) are summarised in the tables in the Appendix \ref{App03} of this study. They report the computed changes in land cover classes for each processed image. Compared to the similar studies, the area of Senegal can be divided into eight ecological zones with eleven land use types. Of these, major land use types in Senegal include the savannahs, cultivated lands, forests, and steppes with savannahs as by far dominating land cover type \cite{WOOMER2004625}. In this research, the classification of the land cover types was adopted to the larger extent of the study with details for the coastal area with included mangrove forests.

The cumulative effects of the climate, environmental and anthropogenic factors results in the decreased mangroves colonies which decline in high-salinity waters \cite{dieye2013dynamique}. This is also supported in the study of Lebigre who reports the retreat of the mangrove in front of the advance of the tannes \cite{Lebigre}. At the same time, the trend in rainfall recovery in West Senegal since 1990s \cite{andrieu2020botanical} resulted in the rehabilitation in mangroves which restored their colonies accordingly, which is visible on the images. This corresponds with the results of the previous studies on the location and distribution of \emph{Rhizophora mangle} and \emph{Avicennia germinans} mangrove species in west Senegal \cite{Diaw,rs13101961}. Earlier studies also report the increase in mangrove trees in the estuaries of Senegal since to recent time \cite{FENT2019214}. Changes in land cover types in Senegal continue the existing fluctuations in vegetation patterns as a result of long-term landscape evolution in West Africa, as noted in several studies earlier \cite{Furian,033139ar,LOUM2014100,BRINK2009501}.

The main triggers for changing types of vegetation and landscape patterns of the Senegalese Sahel region include the climatic factors and anthropogenic activities which resulted in the decline and local extinction of woody species \cite{land3030770}. Major human-induced activities include the dynamical agribusiness in the agricultural commodities which includes the increase of croplands and areas of irrigated vegetation \cite{land8030042}. Such activities are vital for local population and the support of living. Nevertheless, the environmental consequences on Senegalese landscapes include land degradation and changed land cover types. Besides, land degradation is recently reported in the savanna landscapes of Senegal which correlates to the trends in land use/cover changes and significant decrease in woodlands \cite{Kalema}. 

Variations in land cover types in western Senegal reflect the relationships and links between changing vegetation and soil characteristics which are subject to climatic factors, moisture level, salinity and organic matter, as noted earlier \cite{MANLAY200089}. Moreover, pattern of changes in agriculture land cover types over time correlate with seasonal fluctuations in crop-fallow cycle. The agricultural plantations and irrigated lands are distributed in the sporadic settlements along the populated and semi-urban areas. Earlier studies also noted \cite{BENNOUR2023101370} that climate variability is the dominant factor that lead to the increasing the actual evaporation and as a result, salinisation of the affected lands in the Senegal River basin.

The southern sector of the Senegalese vegetation is characterised by the Sudano-Guinean forest savannah and the Guinean forests \cite{Robequain}. The relics of the Guinean forest are distributed in the northern part of Senegal as a narrow band of landscapes stretching parallel to the Atlantic coast. In the southern and eastern regions off Dakar and around the region of Thiès, there are many species that are abundant in the north of the forest and represented by the scattered trees in the Sudano-Sahelian savannah. To the east of Dakar, the northern silvopastoral zone of Senegal presents a vast silvopastoral zone characterized around the Ferlo region, which is characterised by the extreme aridity with dominating land cover types of shrubby savannah and steppe vegetation \cite{land10070755}. The enclaves of hygrophilous vegetation surrounding the coastal wetlands are located in to the north of Dakar with mangroves as dominating land cover type. Besides, this region also includes the occasional groves of the oil palm as spontaneous landscape facies of the Guinean forests.

\section{Conclusions}

Image processing is widely regarded as one of the most well studied areas in computer vision and environmental applications, yet recognising landscape patches under unconstrained effects from climate change affecting environmental landscapes in western Africa still remains an unsolved problem. Remote sensing data can be successfully applied to identify different stages in vegetation and plant phenology as indicated by time series analysis. Specifically, detecting mangrove losses in coastal ecosystems of Senegal s possible using a sequence of satellite images taken for different years. Automatic landscape mapping and recognition of land cover types of Senegal is challenging, due to the fact that landscapes in semi-arid climate of West Africa typically have large variations of patches, mosaic of vegetation inclusions intermittent with anthropogenic urban zones of the expanding cities.

In an effort to drive research in this field, this paper proposed a series of the experiments on image classification using GRASS GIS scripting approach which served as a means of ranking landscapes as indicators of land cover development in the condition of the unstable environmental setting of western Senegal which is affected by climate change. The presented results confirm that remote sensing data such as Landsat OLI/TIRS images can be effectively used to evaluate the variations in landscapes in West Africa. Scripts of GRASS GIS used as a standard methodological benchmark of automated image processing enabled to focus on the analysis of Senegalese landscapes in the coastal region of Dakar and surroundings through classified series of images. To evaluate the performance of GRASS GIS approach to image processing, size images were analysed for matching and non-matching landscape comparison for different years (2015 to 2023), and the classification accuracy is reported as rejection probability for pixels in each Landsat scene.

The proposed algorithms of GRASS GIS scripts can be easily extended to other regions and environmental applications where satellite image processing is required such as classification of landscapes using multi-temporal set of images. Hence, in this study, an advanced approach of GRASS GIS scripts was  introduced to evaluating environmental properties of landscapes changed under climate effects in West Africa, using 'i.cluster', 'i.maxlik' and other auxiliary GRASS GIS modules.The output results shows the behaviour of land cover types in the coastal landscapes through the changes in patches as a result of the climate effects reacting as driver for desertification and land degradation in vulnerable ecosystems. Moreover, the analysis oft he extent and distribution of the selected landscapes on a series of satellite images and the decrease of minor patches enabled to detect the environmental landscape dynamics in selected region of west Senegal. 

This paper reports the results obtained from experimental investigations on land cover types over Senegal analysed using a short-term time series of the multispectral satellite images Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS obtained from the USGS. The image processing was designed using the GRASS GIS GIS to evaluate the variations in mangrove wetlands and other land cover types in western segment of Senegal around the Cape Verde Peninsula and Dakar surroundings, based on the classification of the remote sensing data on 2015-2023. Specifically, this study reported a GIS-based case of image analysis for analysis of land cover changes in west Senegal. Interactive processing and  scripts adjusted for each scene provided an effective way to process multispectral satellite images. Such automation enabled to represent complex interactions within the landscapes in a short-term perspective (eight years) by accurate machine-based digital image processing. In this way, this study has presented an advanced data-driven method of image analysis for automatically detect changes in land cover types in a selected ROI of Senegal over a recent decade.
